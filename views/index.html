How to tell a real Gauri Shankar

Most fake Gauri Shankars are two ordinary beads glued together. Here is what to look for — on ours, and on any bead you are shown.

  1. The join is wood, not a line. Where the beads meet, the surface flows from one into the other. A glued bead shows a thin seam or a shiny line of glue.
  2. Each bead has its own lines. The natural ridges (mukh) run from top to bottom on each half and meet near the join, never cut off sharply.
  3. The texture is the same everywhere. The bumpy surface carries right into the join. Sanding or polish around the join is a warning sign.
  4. Floating and coin tests are myths. Real beads can float and fakes can sink. Only an X-ray reliably shows the inside of a bead.
